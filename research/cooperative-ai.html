Cooperative capabilities of multi-agent AI systems

I wanted to know whether cooperation has to be designed in, or whether it turns up on its own if you get the incentives right. So I built a small brutal world, dropped some agents in it, and paid them three different ways.

the setup

It's a gridworld with scarce resources and agents that can see and affect each other — I think of it as a Hunger Games arena, which is roughly how it behaves. Agents are trained with PPO, there's communication logic between them, and each configuration gets 50,000 episodes so I'm looking at settled behaviour rather than early noise.

The only thing I changed between runs was how agents get paid:

  • Selfish — you're rewarded for what you personally collect.
  • Cooperative — everyone shares in the group's total.
  • Mixed — a blend of the two.

what I expected

Honestly? I expected the cooperative reward to win. It's the intuitive answer — pay the group for group success and you'll get group behaviour. That's the assumption I went in with, and it's the assumption the experiment spent 50,000 episodes dismantling.

what actually happened

The selfish reward produced the most cooperative behaviour. Not the shared one. That result annoyed me for about a week before it started making sense.

My read is that it's a credit assignment problem. Under a shared reward, an agent that does something genuinely clever gets the same payoff as one that wandered into a wall, because the reward is pooled. The learning signal gets smeared across everyone and the gradient can't tell effort from luck. Under a selfish reward the signal stays sharp — each agent knows exactly which of its own actions paid off — and cooperation still emerges, because in that environment helping others happens to be the profitable strategy.

So cooperation appears when it's instrumentally useful and learnable, not when it's morally encouraged by the reward function. Which is a slightly uncomfortable lesson, and I think a useful one for anyone designing multi-agent incentives.

what I'd push back on myself

This is one environment. Gridworlds are famously happy to hand you results that don't survive contact with anything richer, and "cooperation" here means a specific measured behaviour, not the full human concept. I'd want more environments and more seeds before claiming anything general — and the repo is still moving because I keep going back to it.

where it goes next

This is the piece of work that set my direction. It's why I care about legible reasoning — that's a credit assignment problem too, just with explanations instead of rewards — and it runs directly into my ongoing work on neuroplastic collective intelligence, where the communication structure itself gets to change. A workshop submission is in preparation.
