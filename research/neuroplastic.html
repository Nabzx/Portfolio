Neuroplastic collective intelligence

My dissertation fixed who could talk to whom and varied the rewards. This one does the opposite: let the communication structure itself change, and see what the group becomes.

the question

In most multi-agent setups the communication topology is a design decision — you wire the agents up at the start and that's that. Brains don't work like that. Connections that carry useful signal get stronger, ones that don't fade out. So: if you let a collective of agents rewire itself the same way, does it organise into something better than what you'd have designed by hand?

how it works

  • Adaptive graph communication — the network of who-talks-to-whom is learned, not fixed.
  • Hebbian-inspired plasticity — channels that carry useful information strengthen; idle ones decay. Roughly, fire together, wire together.
  • Dynamic topologies — the interaction structure is allowed to shift mid-training rather than only between runs.

Built on Python, PyTorch and PettingZoo, with NetworkX doing the topology analysis.

measuring "emergence" without hand-waving

This is the part I'm most careful about. Emergence is an easy word to throw around and a hard one to evidence — you can always squint at a plot and see structure. So specialisation and coordination get quantified with graph-theoretic and information-theoretic measures, and everything is compared against fixed-topology baselines. If the plastic version isn't beating a sensible fixed graph, it isn't doing anything, however good the animation looks.

where it's at

Genuinely ongoing — this is the one I'm actively building rather than writing up. It's the long thread connecting my dissertation to what I want to do next: understanding how intelligence emerges from interaction, and eventually doing that as doctoral research. Different angle from TandemRLVR, same underlying interest in what happens between agents rather than inside one.
